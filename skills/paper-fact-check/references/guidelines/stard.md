# Diagnostic accuracy studies (STARD 2015)

Official: STARD 2015 statement and checklist, www.equator-network.org.

**Title and abstract**
- Identified as a study of diagnostic accuracy, reporting at least one accuracy measure (sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, AUC).
- Structured abstract.

**Introduction**
- The intended use and clinical role of the index test; study hypotheses.

**Methods**
- Prospective or retrospective data collection.
- Eligibility criteria; on what basis participants were identified (symptoms, previous tests, registry).
- Where and when participants were identified; whether a consecutive, random or convenience sample.
- The index test and the reference standard in enough detail to replicate; rationale for the reference standard.
- Definition and rationale for positivity cut-offs, and whether they were prespecified or chosen after looking at the data.
- Whether clinical information and reference-standard results were available to those performing or reading the index test, and the reverse.
- Methods for estimating or comparing accuracy; handling of indeterminate results and missing data; analyses of variability.
- Intended sample size and how it was determined.

**Results**
- Flow of participants (a diagram is expected); baseline characteristics; distribution of disease severity and alternative diagnoses.
- Time interval and any treatment between index test and reference standard.
- A cross tabulation of index test results (or their distribution) by reference standard results.
- Accuracy estimates with 95% CI; adverse events from either test.

**Discussion and other**
- Limitations including bias and imprecision; implications for practice and the intended use.
- Registration, protocol access, funding.

**Check especially**: sensitivity and specificity can be recomputed from the 2x2 table; thresholds chosen on the test data are labelled as such; the population matches the intended use.
