# Observational studies: cohort, case-control, cross-sectional (STROBE)

Official: STROBE statement and checklists, www.strobe-statement.org.

**Title and abstract**
- The design is named in the title or abstract; the abstract is an informative, balanced summary.

**Introduction**
- Background and rationale; specific objectives and any prespecified hypotheses.

**Methods**
- Design stated early.
- Setting, locations and relevant dates: recruitment, exposure, follow-up, data collection.
- Participants: eligibility criteria, sources and methods of selection; follow-up methods (cohort); case and control selection and matching criteria (case-control).
- Variables clearly defined: outcomes, exposures, predictors, potential confounders, effect modifiers; diagnostic criteria.
- Data sources and measurement methods for each variable; comparability across groups.
- Efforts to address potential sources of bias.
- How the study size was arrived at.
- How quantitative variables were handled and grouped, and why.
- Statistical methods: confounding control, subgroups and interactions, missing data, loss to follow-up (cohort) or matching (case-control), sensitivity analyses.

**Results**
- Numbers at each stage: potentially eligible, examined, confirmed eligible, included, completing follow-up, analysed; reasons for non-participation (a flow diagram is recommended).
- Descriptive characteristics; number with missing data for each variable; follow-up time.
- Outcome events or summary measures.
- Main results: unadjusted and adjusted estimates with 95% CI; which confounders were adjusted for and why; category boundaries when continuous variables were categorised; absolute risk when relevant.
- Other analyses: subgroups, interactions, sensitivity.

**Discussion**
- Key results; limitations with the direction and size of likely bias; cautious interpretation considering multiplicity and other studies; generalisability.

**Other**
- Funding and the role of funders.

**Check especially**: causal language is not used for associations; adjusted and unadjusted results are both shown; the confounders adjusted for match those named in Methods; missing data are counted, not just "handled".
